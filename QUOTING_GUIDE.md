## How We Price Every Job

Every quote at Leaf Cleaning is built on a single, consistent rule: **we price by the pane, not by the window.** Every individual piece of glass on a house counts as one pane. A standard double-hung window with a fixed top section and a sliding bottom section equals **two panes**. This approach eliminates guesswork, keeps every quote consistent regardless of who is standing in the driveway, and protects our margins on every job.

We organize our pricing into six **pane-range tiers**. Rather than calculating a custom price for every house, we match the total pane count to the appropriate tier and charge the rate for the top of that range. A house with 29 panes is quoted at the 40-pane rate — never the 25-pane rate. This ensures we are never undercharging and that our quotes are fast enough to deliver on the spot.

> **The Golden Rule:** Always quote at the top of the range. The tier you land in is the tier you charge.

## Part 1 — Counting Panes Correctly

### Standard Windows

Count every individual piece of glass as one pane. Common examples:

| Window Type | Pane Count |
|---|---|
| Single fixed pane (picture window) | 1 pane |
| Double-hung window (fixed top + sliding bottom) | 2 panes |
| Sliding glass door (two panels) | 2 panes |
| Triple-pane casement unit | 3 panes |
| Skylight | 1 pane |

### French / Divided-Light Windows

French pane windows — also called divided-light, grille, or colonial windows — contain many small individual glass sections separated by thin strips of wood or vinyl (muntins). Counting every small pane at full value would make the quote unfairly expensive for the customer. We apply the **French Pane Rule** instead.

> **French Pane Rule:** Count all the small panes in the French window, then multiply that number by **0.4** to get the equivalent standard pane count used for pricing.

This multiplier reflects the fact that each small French pane takes less time to clean than a full standard pane, while still compensating the company fairly for the added complexity of the divided-light pattern.

**French Pane Conversion Table**

| French Panes Counted | × 0.4 | Equivalent Standard Panes |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | × 0.4 | 2 |
| 10 | × 0.4 | 4 |
| 20 | × 0.4 | 8 |
| 30 | × 0.4 | 12 |
| 40 | × 0.4 | 16 |
| 50 | × 0.4 | 20 |
| 60 | × 0.4 | 24 |
| 80 | × 0.4 | 32 |
| 100 | × 0.4 | 40 |

### Quoting a Mixed House (Standard + French Panes)

1. Count all **standard panes** normally.
2. Count all **French/divided-light panes** and multiply by **0.4**. Round up to the nearest whole number.
3. Add both totals together to get the **total equivalent pane count**.
4. Find the correct pricing tier from the price book below.

**Example:** A house has 22 standard panes and a French door with 20 small panes.

- Standard: 22 panes
- French: 20 × 0.4 = 8 panes
- **Total: 30 panes → Up to 40 Pane tier**

## Part 3 — Service Plans & Discounts

Converting one-time customers into recurring plan customers is the single most important driver of long-term profitability. We offer four service frequencies, each with a corresponding per-visit discount. The discount is always presented as a flat dollar amount — never as a percentage — because a dollar figure sounds more concrete and valuable to the customer.

| Plan | Frequency | Discount Per Visit | Minimum Charge |
|---|---|---|---|
| One-Time | Single visit | $0 off | $125 |
| Biannual | Every 6 months | **$50 off** | $125 |
| Quarterly | Every 3 months | **$100 off** | $125 |
| Monthly | Every month | **$150 off** | $125 |

> **Absolute minimum charge:** No job is ever quoted below **$125**, regardless of any discounts, specials, or combinations applied.

### The Sales Pitch (Word for Word)

When presenting plan options to a customer, always lead with the one-time price and work down. This makes each discount feel like a gift rather than a negotiation.

> *"Our one-time price today is $345. If you do our biannual plan, that drops to $295 — you save $50 every single cleaning. If you go quarterly, it's $245 — you're saving $100 each time we come out, and we keep your windows looking great year-round."*

### Annual Value of a Recurring Customer

The table below shows the annual revenue generated by a single customer on each plan, using a 40-pane exterior-only quote as the example.

| Plan | Price Per Visit | Visits Per Year | Annual Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| One-Time | $345 | 1 | $345 |
| Biannual | $295 | 2 | **$590** |
| Quarterly | $245 | 4 | **$980** |

## Part 5 — Technician Pay & Profitability

We pay technicians on a **20% commission model**. This aligns their incentive directly with company revenue — the more efficiently they work and the more they upsell, the more they earn. There is no cap on technician earnings.

| Tech Level | Panes Per Hour | Revenue Per Hour | Tech Pay Per Hour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Below Average | 10 panes/hr | $80/hr | $16/hr |
| Average | 15 panes/hr | $120/hr | $24/hr |
| Good | 20 panes/hr | $160/hr | $32/hr |
| Pro | 25 panes/hr | $200/hr | $40/hr |

**Target:** Every technician should generate a minimum of **$125 per hour** in revenue. At $8/pane and 16 panes/hr, that target is met.

**Rain Redos:** If a job needs to be redone due to rain, the company pays the technician **$75** and charges the client **$75**. This is a break-even policy designed to protect quality and customer trust without penalizing either party.

**Upsell Commission:** Technicians earn their full 20% commission on all upsells — screens, tracks, interior add-ons — sold on the job. Top performers regularly generate over **$1,000 per week** in upsell revenue alone.
