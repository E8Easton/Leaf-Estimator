## Part 1 — Core Pricing Philosophy

The entire system is built on **pane-based pricing, not window-based pricing.** Every individual piece of glass counts as one pane. A standard double-hung window (fixed top + sliding bottom) = **2 panes.** This consistency protects margins and makes quoting fast in the field.

The business targets **$125/hour in revenue per technician.** At a 20–30% labor cost target, a technician earning $25/hr needs to generate $125/hr in revenue. At a production rate of ~15 panes/hr, that works out to **$8.33/pane**, rounded to **$8.00/pane** as the baseline for custom/large-home quotes. [1][3]

> **The Golden Rule:** Always quote at the top of the range. A house with 29 panes gets charged the 40-pane rate — never the 25-pane rate.

## Part 3 — French Pane Windows (Critical Rule)

French pane windows (also called divided-light or grille windows) contain many small individual panes separated by muntins. Counting every small pane individually would make the quote unfairly expensive for the customer and slow down your quoting process.

### The French Pane Rule: Multiply by 0.4

> **Count all the small panes in the French window, then multiply the total by 0.4 to get the equivalent standard pane count for pricing purposes.** [4]

**Why 0.4?** Each small French pane takes less time to clean than a full standard pane. The 0.4 multiplier accounts for this reduced time while still compensating you fairly for the extra complexity of the divided-light pattern.

### French Pane Calculation Examples

| French Pane Count | × 0.4 | Equivalent Standard Panes |
|-------------------|-------|--------------------------|
| 10 small panes | × 0.4 | **4 standard panes** |
| 20 small panes | × 0.4 | **8 standard panes** |
| 30 small panes | × 0.4 | **12 standard panes** |
| 50 small panes | × 0.4 | **20 standard panes** |
| 100 small panes | × 0.4 | **40 standard panes** |

### How to Quote a Mixed House (Standard + French Panes)

1. Count all **standard panes** normally (each = 1 pane).
2. Count all **French/divided-light panes** and multiply by **0.4**.
3. Add both totals together.
4. Find the correct pricing tier from the price book above.

**Example:** A house has 20 standard panes + a French door with 24 small panes.
- Standard: 20 panes
- French: 24 × 0.4 = **9.6 → round up to 10 panes**
- **Total: 30 panes → charge the Up to 40 Pane tier ($345 exterior)**

## Part 4 — Service Plan Discount Structure

Converting one-time customers to recurring service plans is the core profitability engine. The discount structure is tiered by frequency. [1][2]

| Plan | Frequency | Discount Per Visit | Sales Pitch Example |
|------|-----------|--------------------|---------------------|
| One-Time | Single visit | $0 | $345 |

> **Sales Script:** "Our one-time price is $345. If you do biannual that drops to $295 — you save $50 every cleaning. If you go quarterly it's $245 — you save $100 every cleaning." [2]

## Part 5 — Profitability Model & Technician Pay

The 20% commission model aligns technician incentives with company profitability. [3]

| Tech Level | Panes/Hour | Revenue/Hour | Tech Pay/Hour |
|------------|-----------|--------------|---------------|
| Below Average | 10 | $80 | $16 |
| Average | 15 | $120 | $24 |
| Good | 20 | $160 | $32 |
| Pro | 25 | $200 | $40 |

**Target:** $125/hour in revenue per technician. A pro tech at 25 panes/hr × $8/pane = $200/hr — well above target.

**Rain Redos:** Pay the tech $75, charge the client $75. Break-even on redos to protect quality and customer trust. [3]

**Upsell incentive:** Technicians keep their 20% commission on all upsells (screens, tracks, etc.). Top performers can generate $1,000+ per week in upsells alone. [3]

## Part 6 — Full Quote Example (Field Use)

**Step 1 — Count panes:**
